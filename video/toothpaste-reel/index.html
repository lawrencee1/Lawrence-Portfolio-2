Ancient Romans brushed
their teeth with
pee. And not
just any pee.
They imported it
from Portugal,
because it was
stronger. Egyptians scrubbed
with ox hoof
ashes and crushed
eggshells. Greeks and
Romans? Crushed bones
and oyster shells.
And the first
bristle toothbrush?
Hair from a
pig's neck.
But here's the
crazy part.
A lot of
them had better
teeth than you.
Skeletons from medieval
Norway, a thousand
years ago.
Worn down,
but almost no
cavities. So what
changed? Sugar.
Cavities started with
the rich.
Queen Elizabeth the
First loved marzipan,
and her teeth
paid the price.
Then sugar got
cheap, and everyone's
teeth went downhill.
But not every
ancient smile was
perfect. One Stone
Age group ate
so many acorns,
almost all of
them had rotten
teeth. So yes,
keep brushing.
Toothpaste helps.
But the real
villain was always
sugar. Would you
have survived Roman
dentistry? Tell me
in the comments.
